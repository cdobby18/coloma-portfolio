Still Figuring It Out

Carl Joshua Coloma · June 2026
Tech Life

I'm a computer science student specializing in software engineering, and "aspiring AI engineer" still feels like a big title for someone who spends half his time confused. But that's the goal, so here's the honest version.

I fell into tutorial hell early: course after course, copying along, feeling productive without building anything on my own. Finishing a tutorial isn't the same as understanding it.

I'd rather look clueless for a minute than stay stuck for a month.

The comparison never stops — everyone online seems three steps ahead. What helped was staying curious and asking questions. During my internships in computer vision and AI QA, my co-interns and mentors made even the basic questions feel normal. If I learned something, the mild annoyance was worth it.

I'm not an AI engineer yet. I'm a student becoming one, one confusing concept at a time — learning from mentors and co-interns along the way. If you're in the same spot, you're not the only one.

The Gap Between Watching and Building
← Back to portfolio

The AI engineering journey started with a simple thing: watching. At first, I was just absorbing — hours of professional software engineering and AI content online, the deep technical breakdowns from creators like Gazi, Bryl, and Daniel, full of pipelines, agents, and production-grade thinking.

Most of it went over my head at first. But their breakdowns — the "why" behind the "how" — planted a seed. If they could build these systems, I could start building mine.

You can watch a thousand tutorials, but you only learn by building.

That quote is what finally moved me from passive learning to action. I closed the tabs, opened an editor, and started on my first workflow.

The journey from watching to doing became the real story: inspiration got me curious, but building made it mine.

Still Figuring It Out Beyond the Code: What BAWZ Taught Me
← Back to portfolio

In August 2025 I competed with my dance group BAWZ at an international event in Singapore. We represented the Philippines against teams from across Asia, and we came home with the champion award.

BAWZ is not just a group; it's a team built around discipline, rhythm, and trust. Our coach pushed us through long rehearsals, and every routine had to be precise enough to stand out on an international stage.

The moment we heard that we had won was unforgettable. We had trained for months, and the award validated every injury, every early practice, and every late-night rehearsal.

We took the stage against teams from different countries, and our performance brought home the trophy. It showed what a Philippine crew can do when we combine creativity, precision, and cultural pride.

That win is part of my story too. It taught me how teamwork and preparation pay off, and it gave me confidence that I can represent the Philippines at a high level — whether on a stage, in a project, or in a technical role.
